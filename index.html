Heritage tourism in Australia

How is Australia's heritage recognised, and how do travellers experience it? Start with the national list, then follow tourism participation, activities and a New South Wales case study.

Listed places describe recognition, not visitor attendance. Tourism surveys describe cultural activities, not travellers' identities. Here, “First Nations” refers to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.

From listed places to First Nations tourism

The register does not measure tourism. Surveys add a different view: where First Nations overnight trips go, then how widely international trips include these activities.

Domestic trips are made by Australian residents. International trips are made by overseas visitors. Financial years run from July to June. These measures count trips, not unique travellers.

New South Wales: activities, stays and timing

New South Wales connects the national comparisons. It shares the largest listed-place total and leads the First Nations overnight estimates. Now broaden the lens to culture and heritage tourism.

This group also includes markets and performing arts, not just First Nations activities. These charts describe domestic visitors in calendar 2024, a separate population and period from the national surveys.

Recognition and tourism reveal different sides of heritage. Historic records dominate the selected national list. First Nations tourism spans several destinations and activities, but these surveys do not measure attendance at listed sites.

Visitor groups shape the story. In New South Wales, comparable activity percentages represent different visitor totals. Short stays dominate overnight visits. The year-end lift distinguishes overnight visitors from day-trip visitors.
