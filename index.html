Heritage tourism in Australia

Historic places make up most of the records shown. Cultural participation differs between domestic and international trips. Follow the maps and comparisons to explore listed heritage and travellers' experiences.

First Nations means Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples. The tourism surveys describe trips involving cultural activities, not travellers' identities or attendance at listed sites.

Participation in First Nations tourism

Tourism surveys describe trips involving First Nations activities, not visits to the listed places. Financial years run from July to June.

Domestic trips are made by Australian residents. International trips are made by overseas visitors to Australia.

Culture and heritage tourism in New South Wales

New South Wales, on Australia's east coast, ties Victoria at 30 listed places and reports the largest First Nations overnight average, 240,000 trips per year.

Now we look at its broader domestic culture and heritage group, including markets and performing arts. These separate observations cover January to December 2024, not the national financial years.

65 of the 120 places shown are Historic-classified. About 15% of international trips included a First Nations activity in July 2023 to June 2024. These are separate measures of listed heritage and cultural participation.

In New South Wales, about 63% of overnight culture and heritage visitors stayed one to three nights in 2024. Together, the findings describe different aspects of heritage tourism, not evidence that listed places caused tourism demand.

Sources and methods

Author: Vincent Yoong
Webpage revised: 7 October 2026
Heritage data snapshot: 4 October 2026

Each point is derived from the largest component of its source geometry. Three source locations are generalised. Locations do not establish public access. There are 120 unique places and 124 state memberships because three places cross state boundaries.

The source contains 126 distinct listed places across all regions. Six places coded EXT are excluded to match the eight states and territories shown, leaving 120 distinct places. Repeated feature rows are grouped by PLACE_ID. All register counts and shares use this selected scope.

Sources were retrieved on 4 October 2026. National tourism tables cover financial years through July 2023 to June 2024. State overnight figures average July 2021 to June 2024. New South Wales observations cover calendar 2024 and were published in May 2025. These periods and populations are not interchangeable.

Tourism counts and percentages are reported rounded survey estimates. Register counts are counts of listed records. Four unresolved regional participation rates are excluded. Two source rounded-total discrepancies are retained rather than adjusted. Missing historical activities and the suppressed New South Wales festival share remain missing.

Activity questions expanded from 2022. The exact rollout month is not reported. The timeline therefore breaks before July 2021 to June 2022, the first financial year containing 2022. Both observations on either side of the break remain visible.
